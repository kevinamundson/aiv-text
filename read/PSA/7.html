Psalm 7 DRAFT

I Take Refuge in You

A Shiggaiona of David, which he sang to YHWH, concerning the words of Cush, the Benjamite.

1 O YHWH my God, in You I have taken refuge;

keep me safe from all those pursuing me, and deliver me,

2lest they tear my soul like a lion,

tearing apart, and there is no one snatching me up.

3O YHWH my God, if I have done this,

if there is injustice in my palms,

4if I have dealt with the one at peace with meb with evil,

or have drawn out my enemy without cause,

5may the enemy pursue my soul and overtake it,

and let him trample my life to the ground,

and let him set down my honor in the dust.

Selah

6arise, O YHWH, in Your anger;

lift up against the fury of my adversaries,

and rouse Yourself for me; You have appointed a judgment.

7And let the congregation of peoples surround You;

and over it return in the heights.

8YHWH shall judge the peoples;

Judge me, O YHWH, according to my righteousness,

and according to my integrity within me.

9Please, let the evil of the wicked cease,

and may you establish the righteous—

even the One testing hearts and kidneys, O Righteous God.

10My shield is on God,

the One saving the upright of heart.

11God is a righteous judge,

and a God expressing wrath on every day.

12If one shall not turn back,

then He sharpens His sword;

He has bent His bow and prepared it.

13And for Himself He has prepared instruments of death;

His arrows He makes into fiery shafts.

14Behold, he travails with evil,

and he conceives trouble, and brings forth falsehood.

15He has dug a pit and hollowed it out,

and he has fallen into a pit he has made.

16His trouble shall return upon his head,

and on the top of his head, his violence shall come down.

17I will confess YHWH according to His righteousness,

and I will sing praise to the name of YHWH-Elyon.c

For the one directing. According to Gittith.d

Notes

  1. a 7.1 Shiggaion is probably a musical or liturgical term.
  2. b 7.4 Or with my ally
  3. c 7.17 YHWH-Elyon means LORD Most High
  4. d 7.17 Gittith is probably a musical or liturgical term; here and in Psalms 81 and 84.
